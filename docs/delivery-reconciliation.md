# Submission, delivery and usage completeness

Twinevia retains three different facts for an outbound SID: the provider accepted the submission, a delivery receipt established a result, and provider cost/segment facts became complete. An accepted request or durable `sent` attempt continues to mean provider acceptance. A later delivery failure never changes that attempt, creates a replacement send, changes a cancellation, or rewrites the original log success counters.

Twilio's `sent` status means upstream carrier acceptance and does not establish recipient delivery. Status callbacks can arrive out of order, and message price/segment data can arrive later than the original API response. The implementation follows the [Twilio Message resource](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/api/message-resource), [status callback guide](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/track-outbound-message-status) and [provider price timing guidance](https://help.twilio.com/articles/46871410243099-Understanding-SMS-Message-Fees-Carrier-Fees-and-Phone-Number-Fees).

`MessagingUsageRecord` remains unique per provider SID. It now retains independent delivery status/error/receipt time, cost status, segment completeness, receipt and usage-fact deadlines, a next reconciliation time, attempt count, deduplicated status evidence, and an applied-suppression marker. `reconciliation_status` is a compatibility aggregate, not a delivery-success claim. `finalized` can include an explicitly unconfirmed delivery receipt after the receipt window expires, provided economic facts are complete. `unknown` cost/units remains visibly incomplete and cannot be automatically settled as a verified zero.

Delivery progress only advances through pending/accepted/queued/sending/carrier-sent states. A terminal delivered/read/undelivered/failed/canceled receipt survives later stale queued or sent observations. Duplicate status/error observations retain one evidence entry and one suppression action. Contradictory authenticated terminal receipts are preserved as `conflict` for manual investigation; they do not silently become delivered. A late authentic receipt can improve an aged-out `unknown` delivery result.

New SaaS submissions provide a message-specific callback URL at `/webhooks/twilio/delivery?organization_id=<id>`. The endpoint always verifies the Twilio SDK signature over the URL and form, requires a recognized organization profile, and binds the known SID to the retained organization/provider account. Sending readiness and subscription suspension do not block valid receipt ingestion. Unknown SIDs do not invent usage records: a callback can race the initial ledger commit, and normal per-SID polling supplies the fallback. No provider messaging-service or number resources are edited to enable this behavior.

Polling uses the organization's retained read credentials even when sending is error/suspended. The fetched SID, account and outbound direction are checked before merging. Provider network I/O occurs before the usage row lock; the locked refresh merges the callback/poll result and commits inbox projection and failure suppression atomically. The original log JSON stays intact; log views overlay the current per-SID receipt in a separate delivery column, and inbox bubbles show current receipt/error text. Delivery revision changes are independent of cost-only polling.

The defaults are `DELIVERY_RECEIPT_TIMEOUT_HOURS=72`, `USAGE_FACTS_TIMEOUT_DAYS=7`, `USAGE_RECONCILE_BATCH_SIZE=100`, and `USAGE_RECONCILE_TIME_BUDGET_SECONDS=30`. Polling backs off from one minute to one hour and is bounded by batch size and a wall-clock budget, in addition to explicit socket timeouts: `USAGE_PROVIDER_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=10` for message reads and `TWILIO_MESSAGE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=30` for submissions (both capped at 60 seconds; an already stricter client timeout is preserved). The installed SDK otherwise defaults to an unbounded HTTP timeout. These bounds apply to message reads/submissions and do not modify provider provisioning/resource workflows. Deadlines begin with tracking and are tightened to the provider's actual creation time when that timestamp is fetched. Historical messages get a fresh provider read, then a missing receipt/price already outside its window becomes explicit unknown. This does not claim that an unconfirmed message was delivered or not delivered. Expired facts stop automatic polling; an intentional `reconcile_messaging_usage(organization_id=<id>, include_expired=True)` can recheck a selected organization. Late signed receipts remain accepted without re-enabling a send.

SaaS migration 023 is additive and idempotent. It reopens historical finalized `sent` and incomplete-economic rows without modifying recipient, request, attempt, invoice or cancellation evidence. Historical nonzero provider amounts and positive units retain their observed facts; historical zero cost is not treated as proof that the provider supplied zero. Previously incomplete economic rows are flagged `billing_review_required`, and their first recovery records prior cost, units, sell amount and period identity in a `usage_historical_review` provider audit entry. Existing period links are preserved. No recovery flag is silently cleared.

Settlement eligibility requires known provider cost, known segment units, a verified assigned period, and the expected currency; it does not require a successful delivery receipt. Unresolved or historical-review rows hold an unposted period in `review_required`. A positive period with missing underlying usage evidence also requires review. Existing invoiced/posted/Stripe-linked period snapshots are frozen. Late variance creates a `usage_settled_variance` audit incident and cannot change settled units, amounts or invoice identities. Migration and reconciliation therefore do not authorize a historical charge, correction invoice, refund or additional overage. A separate operator decision must resolve such incidents with retained evidence.

Focused verification covers queued → sent → delivered/undelivered, late and explicit-zero price, incomplete initial segments, receipt/cost age-out, late receipts, invalid signature/tenant/account/SID, paused organizations, read-only provider selection, duplicate/out-of-order/conflicting observations, atomic failure/retry, rendered log/inbox receipts, migration replay, historical review holds, settled snapshot preservation, and real PostgreSQL callback/poll races. All providers are mocked and non-local socket access is blocked during tests. Test PostgreSQL targets are explicitly limited to unique UTF8 disposable databases on the local audit server; production provider resources are not used by the tests.
